## Selection rationale

Atiku Abubakar is selected for methodological diversity, not political preference. The first two validated dossiers are materially different: Bola Ahmed Tinubu is a former Lagos governor and current federal president; Peter Gregory Obi is a former Anambra governor whose dossier stresses judicially resolved state office history, state fiscal evidence and rapid recent party transitions. Atiku adds a distinct federal executive history: he was elected Governor of Adamawa State in 1998 but became Vice President before taking office, served as Vice President from 1999–2007, and has a long multi-election presidential history. He also introduces a current 2027 candidacy whose party/legal status requires active temporal and institutional verification.

## Methodological difference from Tinubu

1. Former Vice President rather than a former state governor who later became President.
2. Governor-elect status followed by selection as a presidential running mate before assuming the governorship, creating a distinct office/candidacy transition case.
3. Multiple presidential primary/general-election cycles across different parties and eras, requiring repeated election-specific candidacy records.
4. A major federal policy/economic record during the Obasanjo administration, including National Council on Privatisation activity, creating a federal rather than state-level economic/policy evidence problem.
5. Current ADC candidacy creates a live party-registration and nomination-state issue that must be represented temporally rather than treated as static.

## Methodological difference from Peter Obi

1. Atiku's core executive record is federal vice-presidential rather than state-governorship performance.
2. His political history spans a much longer period and substantially more presidential contests.
3. The current 2027 candidacy is on ADC, while Obi's current 2027 candidacy is on NDC; the two therefore provide a useful test of divergent contemporary party histories after their earlier shared ADC involvement.
4. Atiku's office history contains a governor-elect → vice-president transition rather than Obi's court-resolved governorship and later state officeholding.
5. His economic/policy evidence includes federal privatisation and national-level policy claims, requiring tighter distinction between documented institutional action and claims about economic effects.

## Expected research challenges

1. Reconstructing long-span party and candidacy intervals without conflating primary contests, general-election candidacies and officeholding.
2. Separating Atiku's own public statements from factual propositions contained in those statements.
3. Distinguishing the 1998 Adamawa governorship election, governor-elect status and the subsequent vice-presidential appointment.
4. Establishing the exact source lineage for 1999–2007 federal policy and economic claims.
5. Handling multiple presidential election records, including 1993, 2007, 2011, 2015, 2019 and 2023, with election-specific party and result records.
6. Representing the 2027 ADC candidacy while preserving the legal history surrounding the party's attempted deregistration and subsequent appellate proceedings.
7. Finding reproducible quantitative observations with definitions, periods, geography, dataset versions and methodology rather than relying on retrospective political scorecards.
8. Capturing relevant public-conversation artifacts while preserving uncertainty where platform availability, deletion or authenticity is incomplete.
