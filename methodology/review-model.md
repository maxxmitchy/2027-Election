# Review Model

Reviews are independent assessments of records or generated outputs. They are not evidence by default and cannot increase factual truth by popularity.

## Dimensions

A review may assess independently:

- **Evidence quality:** whether the cited evidence is relevant, sufficient and properly connected to the claim.
- **Factual accuracy:** whether the record accurately represents the underlying facts/source.
- **Calculation accuracy:** whether arithmetic, units, inputs and formulas are correct and reproducible.
- **Context completeness:** whether material context, dates, scope, caveats or contradictory evidence is missing.
- **Source quality:** whether the source is appropriate, authentic, inspectable and methodologically fit.
- **Reviewer confidence:** how confident the reviewer is in the review itself.

A reviewer may leave a dimension unassessed. No numeric score is required.

## Review outcome

The categorical review outcome is separate from the dimensions. A review can flag an incorrect calculation while still finding the underlying source strong. Conversely, a high-quality source does not make an inaccurate interpretation correct.

## Independence and history

Reviews retain reviewer identity according to governance/privacy policy, timestamp, target version, rationale and proposed action. Subsequent adjudication creates a new record version; the original review remains immutable.

## Popularity

Review counts, agreement rates and community ratings are metadata about review activity. They must never be represented as evidence quality, source reliability or probability that a claim is true.
