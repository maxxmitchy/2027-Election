# Verification Rules

## Verification statuses

- `unverified`: source located but material claim not yet checked
- `source-checked`: source inspected and claim accurately reflects what it says
- `corroborated`: independently supported by additional evidence
- `disputed`: credible evidence materially conflicts
- `refuted`: available evidence supports a conclusion contrary to the claim
- `insufficient`: available evidence does not justify a reliable conclusion
- `superseded`: validly replaced by a later version or corrected source

## Minimum standard

Material factual claims should be source-checked. High-impact claims should normally be corroborated where practical. Numerical claims should preserve source definition, unit, date/period and calculation.

## Contradictions

Contradictory evidence is retained and linked. Researchers must not select only supporting evidence while omitting material contrary evidence.

## Causality

Temporal change and causal attribution are separate fields. A metric changing during an administration does not establish that the administration caused the change. Causal conclusions require an explicit methodology and substantially stronger evidence than a before/after comparison.

## Confidence

Confidence assesses the record as currently supported. It should consider source quality, independence, corroboration, contradiction, completeness, methodological fit and recency.
