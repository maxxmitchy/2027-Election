# Source Rating Methodology

Source tiers describe evidentiary provenance, not political agreement or absolute truth.

## Tier 1 — Primary authoritative records

Legislation, court judgments, official election records, government statistical releases, audited reports, official filings, regulatory decisions and direct statements or documents issued by the relevant person or institution.

## Tier 2 — Strong institutional or evidentiary sources

Academic research, reputable institutional datasets, professional investigations and reporting that identifies and permits inspection of underlying evidence.

## Tier 3 — Established reporting

Reputable journalism, interviews and reporting with identifiable authorship and sourcing.

## Tier 4 — Secondary aggregation

Aggregators, summaries and derivative databases where the underlying evidence is less direct or transparent.

## Tier 5 — Unverified material

Unverified social-media claims, anonymous assertions, unattributed screenshots and material whose provenance cannot be established.

## Rating rules

A tier is an input to confidence, not a substitute for verification. A Tier 1 source can contain an error; a lower-tier source can surface information unavailable in a primary record. Records should explain why a source is being relied upon when the evidentiary choice is non-obvious.

For social-media evidence, distinguish evidence that a person made a statement from evidence supporting the truth of the statement's underlying proposition.
