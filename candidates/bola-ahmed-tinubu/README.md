# Bola Ahmed Tinubu — Gate 4 Pilot Dossier

**Pilot status:** controlled research fixture, not a complete public biography.

## Identity

Bola Ahmed Tinubu is the current President of Nigeria and the APC's nominated presidential candidate for the 2027 election as of the research cutoff of 30 August 2026. The State House identifies him as President and records his presidency as beginning on 29 May 2023. The State House also records his eight-year tenure as Governor of Lagos State from 1999 to 2007. citeturn0search0turn3search1

The APC's published biography states that Tinubu attended Richard J. Daley College and Chicago State University and that Chicago State University confirmed his attendance and 1979 graduation in its own 2023 statement. citeturn2search0turn1search1

## Political chronology

- 1992: elected to the Senate for Lagos West on the Social Democratic Party platform, according to the APC biography and independent historical reporting. citeturn2search0turn2search7
- 1999: elected Governor of Lagos State on the Alliance for Democracy platform. citeturn2search0
- 2003: re-elected Governor of Lagos State; the available result used in this pilot is a secondary-source result and is therefore not treated as an authoritative electoral anchor. citeturn1search11
- 2007: completed his second Lagos governorship term. citeturn0search0turn2search1
- 2013: became associated with the formation of the All Progressives Congress through the merger of ACN, CPC, ANPP and an APGA faction. citeturn2search3
- 2023: APC presidential candidate; INEC declared him elected with 8,794,726 votes. citeturn1search88
- 2026: APC nominated Tinubu as its presidential flagbearer for the 2027 election. citeturn3search1turn3search6

## Economic pilot

Two directly reproducible inflation comparisons are included in the machine-readable records:

- December 2022 → December 2023 headline inflation: 21.34% → 28.92%, a **7.58 percentage-point increase**. citeturn0search137
- December 2023 → January 2024 headline inflation: 28.92% → 29.90%, a **0.98 percentage-point increase**. citeturn0search12

The pilot deliberately does not describe these changes as being caused by Tinubu merely because they occurred during his presidency.

For GDP, NBS reports 2023 annual real GDP growth of 2.74%; a later NISER report citing NBS reports 2024 growth of 3.40%. The pilot treats the latter as a derived comparison requiring dataset/version provenance rather than as proof of policy causation. citeturn4search61turn4search60

For public debt, the DMO reports total public debt of ₦97.341 trillion at 31 December 2023. The pilot preserves the observation and does not infer that the president caused the change in debt. citeturn4search59

For FX, the CBN describes its 14 June 2023 willing-buyer/willing-seller reform and subsequent NFEM framework. The pilot records the policy event separately from exchange-rate observations so temporal sequence is not converted into causal attribution. citeturn0search10turn0search9

## Policy/event example

The 14 June 2023 CBN FX-market reform is represented as a policy/event record. The CBN says the reform was intended to unify market segments and improve price discovery. This is recorded as the institution's documented description of the policy, not as an independently established causal outcome. citeturn0search10

## Legal record

The pilot includes Supreme Court case **SC/CV/501/2023**, delivered 26 May 2023, concerning a challenge involving the APC nomination and Bola Tinubu/Kashim Shettima. The record separates allegations made by litigants from the court's procedural/judicial outcome. citeturn1search6turn1search8

## Social-media semantics

A material statement from the verified `@officialABAT` account is preserved as evidence that the account published the statement. The proposition in that post is not automatically treated as established fact. citeturn5search1

## Contradictory / qualifying evidence

The administration and its representatives describe the reforms as improving macroeconomic stability and resilience, while the IMF states that reforms improved macroeconomic outcomes but that conditions remain difficult for many Nigerians, with substantial poverty and food-insecurity concerns. These records are represented as **qualifying/contextualizing evidence**, not as a forced binary choice. citeturn0search4turn6search5

## Uncertainty

The pilot explicitly marks unavailable or insufficiently authoritative historical material as `UNKNOWN`, `INCOMPLETE`, or `UNVERIFIED` rather than filling gaps by inference.

## Source discipline

The authoritative anchors used here are primarily State House, INEC, NBS, CBN, DMO, Chicago State University, IMF and the Supreme Court case record as available through inspected sources. Secondary sources are retained where they add historical context or where a primary historical record was not located, but their lower evidentiary status is preserved.

Machine-readable records are in `candidates/bola-ahmed-tinubu/data/` and the acceptance validator is `tests/gate4_candidate_acceptance.py`.
