## Selection rationale

Peter Gregory Obi is the second controlled candidate because his research conditions differ materially from the Bola Ahmed Tinubu pilot. Obi is a former state governor rather than a current federal officeholder; his Anambra office history includes a court-determined 2003 election outcome and non-contiguous early officeholding; his documented party history spans APGA, PDP, Labour Party, ADC and NDC; and he is a 2027 presidential candidate on the NDC after contesting the 2023 presidential election on the Labour Party platform. This makes him a useful stress test for temporal party membership, judicially determined officeholding, candidacy/result normalization, contradictory fiscal evidence and volatile public-conversation provenance.

## Identity

Peter Gregory Obi was born on 19 July 1961 in Onitsha, Anambra State, Nigeria. A parliamentary research briefing records a BA in Philosophy from the University of Nigeria, Nsukka and identifies him as a former Governor of Anambra State who served two terms from March 2006 to March 2014.

## Political and office chronology

- 2003: APGA candidate for Anambra State governor. The election was initially declared for Chris Ngige; subsequent litigation resulted in the declaration being set aside and Obi being sworn in on 17 March 2006.
- 17 March 2006: sworn in as Governor of Anambra State following the judicial resolution of the 2003 election dispute.
- 2007: office history was interrupted by judicial and constitutional developments; the Anambra State Government's historical list records periods in office in 2006–2007 and a subsequent return after court proceedings.
- 2010: won the Anambra governorship election on the APGA platform with approximately 97,843 votes according to contemporary reporting of the INEC declaration.
- 17 March 2014: left office as Governor of Anambra State; Willie Obiano was sworn in the same day.
- January 2014: appointed APGA National Leader and Chairman of its Board of Trustees; resigned those positions in August 2014.
- October 2014: joined the PDP.
- October 2018: selected as Atiku Abubakar's running mate for the 2019 presidential election on the PDP ticket.
- March/May 2022: left PDP and joined the Labour Party; subsequently won the Labour Party presidential primary for the 2023 election.
- 2023: contested the presidential election on the Labour Party ticket and received 6,101,533 votes according to INEC's official general-election report.
- December 2025: formally joined the ADC.
- 3 May 2026: left ADC and formally joined the NDC.
- 30 May 2026: affirmed as NDC presidential candidate for the 2027 election.

## Election history

### 2003 Anambra governorship

The 2003 election is treated as a judicially resolved election rather than a simple historical result field. Contemporary and judicial records establish that Chris Ngige was initially declared winner, the declaration was challenged, the election tribunal set the declaration aside, and appellate proceedings upheld the outcome leading to Obi's swearing-in on 17 March 2006.

The dossier therefore preserves the initial declaration, litigation and eventual officeholding separately rather than rewriting the historical record as though the initial declaration never existed.

Sources:
- https://nigerialii.org/akn/ng/judgment/ngsc/2007/42/eng@2007-06-13
- https://nigerialii.org/akn/ng/judgment/ngsc/2007/171/eng@2007-07-12

### 2010 Anambra governorship

Peter Obi won the 6 February 2010 Anambra governorship election on the APGA platform. Contemporary reporting of the INEC declaration records 97,833 votes; other reproductions of the result record 97,843. The discrepancy is retained as a source-normalization issue rather than silently choosing one number. The authoritative electoral artifact remains the preferred target for final validation.

### 2023 presidential election

INEC's official 2023 General Election Report records Peter Obi, Labour Party, with 6,101,533 votes. The result is represented as an official election result linked to the 2023 presidential candidacy, not as a property of the person.

## Economic record — initial controlled dataset

The economic section will prioritize state-level fiscal observations during the period relevant to Obi's governorship rather than incorrectly importing national indicators into a state-level administration comparison.

A key controlled observation is the Debt Management Office's 31 December 2013 Anambra debt position:

- domestic debt: ₦3,025,797,046.67
- external debt: US$30,323,574.40

These are observations about Anambra State's debt stock. They do not by themselves establish which administration incurred each liability or whether Obi caused the debt position.

## Contradictory evidence

A controlled contradiction concerns statements about whether Obi left Anambra State with debt. Obi's former media aide stated in 2015 that the state did not borrow under Obi and had not raised bonds, while the DMO's 2013 records show Anambra with domestic and external debt stock. A later fact-check characterized the broader “owed nobody” formulation as misleading and distinguished formal public debt from other liabilities.

The repository will preserve the statement, the DMO observations and the fact-check as separate evidence with typed relationships. It will not collapse them into a single binary political judgment.

## Uncertainty and limitations

1. The 2010 Anambra result has a small numerical discrepancy across accessible reproductions (97,833 versus 97,843). The final dossier will seek the original INEC result artifact before assigning an authoritative vote count.
2. Historical party membership dates require temporal evidence and should not be inferred solely from candidacy dates.
3. State fiscal observations require scope discipline: state debt cannot be directly compared with federal debt without a defined analytical framework.
4. Social-media material is volatile; original-post availability and archive capture will be recorded separately from the statement proposition.
5. No causal conclusion about Obi's governorship is made from the initial economic observations.
